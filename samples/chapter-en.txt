Chapter 147 — The One Who Counts Steps

The master did not turn around. He stood at the edge of the drop, where the wind stripped dry dust from the stones, and counted his breaths — the same way he had counted them thirty years ago, climbing this path himself.

"You are four heartbeats late," he said.

Lin Wei said nothing. Arguing with a man who could hear another's heart through three layers of cloth would have been foolish, and the Ninefold Breath Seal on his wrist had not yet cooled from the climb.

"Then you will count for me," said Master Gu. "Until you can tell me how many steps lie between the gate and this stone."

The boy took the first step before he understood that he had already begun.

By the second morning the count had stopped being a number. It sat somewhere below his ribs, rising and falling with the air, and when he lost it he did not lose a figure but a footing — the whole mountain tilted a little until he found the rhythm again.

Master Gu did not look at him once. In three days he said nothing at all, and the silence weighed more than any lesson the Silent Fang Sect had ever set him. Lin Wei had been beaten in the practice yard, made to hold water above his head until his arms shook loose, sent up Cloudfall Ridge in a storm with nothing but a rope. None of it had been as heavy as an old man sitting on a rock, not watching.

On the fourth morning Elder Sister Xiao came down the path with a bowl of millet and a question she did not ask. She set the bowl on the flat stone, looked at the master, looked at Lin Wei, and went back the way she had come. The bowl was still warm when he reached it, which meant she had walked fast, which meant she had wanted to be gone before anyone spoke.

"Nine hundred and forty," Lin Wei said, on the sixth day.

"No."

He began again.

He did not ask what he had counted wrong. He had learned by then that the answer to that question was always the same, and that it was never about the number. Somewhere between the gate and the stone he was still walking like a boy in a hurry, and the mountain was patient enough to say so for as long as it took.

"Nine hundred and forty," he said, eleven days later.

Master Gu turned around for the first time. His eyes were the colour of wet slate, and there was nothing kind in them, and nothing cruel either.

"Now," he said, "walk it again, and tell me which one of them is the step that matters."

The wind came up over the edge and took the dust with it. Below, very far below, the roofs of the sect held the last of the light like water in a shallow bowl. Lin Wei looked at them for a moment, and then he turned back towards the gate, and he began to walk.
